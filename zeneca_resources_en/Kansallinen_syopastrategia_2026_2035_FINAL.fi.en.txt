National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035
Summary
The cancer burden among Finns will increase 
significantly over the next ten years. As the population 
ages, the need for cancer treatment will also increase 
significantly. Cancer affects almost all Finns in some 
way, and more and more people are living with or 
recovering from cancer. Cancer and cancer treatments 
have a significant impact on the quality of life and 
functional capacity of those affected.
The aim of the National Cancer Strategy is to 
improve equality and quality of life for patients, 
taking into account their loved ones. Cancer 
prevention and reducing cancer deaths are also key 
objectives.
The Cancer Strategy creates a strong foundation 
for Finland to respond to the growing cancer burden 
and become an international pioneer in cancer 
prevention, treatment and research.
Cancer is not a single disease, but a group of 
hundreds of different types of diseases. Their diagnostics, 
treatment and rehabilitation touch almost all areas of 
healthcare, from primary healthcare to various fields of 
specialized medical care. Therefore, cancer treatment, 
rehabilitation and individual psychosocial support require 
an increasingly multidisciplinary approach as well as 
more individual molecular diagnostics and treatment 
based on it. These services must be available equally and 
without delay throughout the country.
The first national cancer strategy Together against 
cancer – People-centered cancer prevention and 
treatmentidentifies the key challenges of the next ten 
years and presents solutions to them. The steering group 
for the strategy work has included the client, the Ministry 
of Social Affairs and Health (STM), the Finnish National 
Cancer Institute (FICAN), which was responsible for 
implementing the work, as well as cancer organisations 
and the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare (THL).
The strategy includes four main objectives:
1.Strengthening inclusion and developing people-
oriented services
2.Reducing the burden of cancer through prevention 
and early detection
3.Ensuring equal and effective cancer treatment
4.On the crest of a wave in a changing operating 
environment
In addition, equality has been raised as a cross-cutting 
theme across all goals, taking into account the 
diagnostics and treatment of elderly cancer patients in 
particular, but also other population, age and disease 
groups. The key values   that influenced the selection of 
goals have been the reduction of inequality, quality of 
life, the effectiveness of treatment and the importance of 
research.
The strategy is based on extensive working group work, 
in which concrete and comprehensive proposals for 
measures were defined for national goals under the 
leadership of FICAN. The working groups included more than 
200 experts from healthcare, research and education 
organizations, and NGOs. In addition, the strategy and its 
goals were discussed in a national expert network, which also 
included a wide range of business representatives. The 
parties involved in the strategy work are described in 
Appendix 1. The strategy was finalized during autumn 2025 
based on feedback collected in the Ota kantaa service.
The strategy describes the measures and 
responsibilities that will guide the achievement of the 
goals over a five-year period. Clear indicators and 
quantitative targets have been defined for monitoring 
(Figure 1), which are to be achieved in stages by 2035.

The integration of primary and specialized 
healthcare in welfare areas creates new opportunities 
to detect cancers at an earlier stage. It also helps to 
systematically collect cancer data to support effective 
treatment, giving Finland a strong position at the 
forefront of research, development and innovation 
(RDI) using health data.
The implementation of the strategy will be launched under 
the coordination of the National Cancer Centre, FICAN. A 
roadmap supporting the implementation of the strategy is 
already being prepared and will be published during 2026. The 
necessary and sufficient mandate for the long-term 
implementation of the cancer strategy must be ensured
It is important that the state and the welfare regions 
are strongly committed to implementing the 
strategy.
All cancer stakeholders will participate in the 
implementation and cooperation in this will be promoted 
by the Cancer Mission Hub Finland, which will be 
established in 2026. This national actor is part of a 
European network that aims to promote the EU's cancer 
mission and the cancer control plan at the national level. 
In Finland, these goals are in line with the cancer 
strategy: to ensure the best possible quality of life for 
those affected, to promote equal access to cost-effective 
treatments and to harmonise national recommendations 
for cancer treatment.

Current status and operating environment
Cancer in Finland
Based on international comparisons, cancer 
prevention and treatment in Finland are currently at 
an excellent level overall. At the same time, however, 
we have lagged behind other Nordic countries in 
terms of survival rates for some cancers, and new 
challenges lie ahead over the next ten years. The 
expected number of cancer cases in Finland is 
growing particularly rapidly as the population ages. 
Cancer prevention, diagnostics and treatment are 
developing rapidly, which offers opportunities for 
more targeted treatments, but causes cost pressure. 
In this situation, it has been considered that Finland 
needs a strategy specifically for cancer that takes into 
account other national development projects.
The number of new cancer cases is predicted to 
increase by about 20% in just over ten years without 
additional measures. This means an increase in the 
number of cases from the current slightly less than 
38,000 to almost 45,000 cancer cases in 2035. About 
a third of those diagnosed with cancer are of working 
age. Early diagnosis, ambitious treatment and 
rehabilitation of this patient group back to working 
life are also important from the perspective of 
society's dependency ratio.
More and more people with cancer are living 
longer. In 2035, it is expected that there will be as 
many as 480,000 people living with or surviving 
cancer in Finland, which represents a 35% increase 
from 2022.

The increase in cancers is particularly concentrated in 
older people, and more and more cancer patients are 
over 75 years old – often with multiple diseases and 
frailty. Cancer treatments for people over 75 years old 
need to be carefully planned due to aging and many 
other diseases, so symptomatic treatment will be 
needed more than at present.
Cancer prevention is the most effective way to 
reduce suffering and costs. It also holds significant 
potential, as a significant proportion of new cancers 
are linked to preventable risk factors. In Finland, 
overweight is currently the most prominent risk 
factor, but smoking and alcohol consumption are 
also significant risk factors. This strategy presents 
concrete measures that can be used to
to address risk factors and prevent cancers also among 
the working-age population.
Detecting cancer at an earlier stage is another 
highly effective way to improve the prognosis and 
burden of cancer for individual cancer patients. The 
strategy takes into account evolving methods of early 
detection and support for primary health care, which 
promote the ability of health care to identify cancers.
Reducing the workload of healthcare personnel is 
essential, as the increase in the need for care and the 
retirement of professionals in the field are already 
reflected in increased staff dissatisfaction and a staff 
shortage in healthcare.

In Finland, the operating environment for cancer 
treatment and research is a broad and multi-level 
entity, divided into healthcare guidance and 
evaluation, prevention and treatment 
implementation, research and education, innovation 
and business activities, and the activities of citizens 
and organizations (Figure 4). The environment is 
multi-channel and decentralized, which has caused 
challenges, especially for guidance, resource 
allocation and the introduction of innovations.
Responsibilities and resources for the implementation of 
cancer prevention, screening, treatment, rehabilitation and 
research are currently divided among several actors. In the 
activities of ministries, the Finnish Institute for Health and 
Welfare, welfare regions and universities, cancer-related 
activities are linked to broader entities and strategies. In the 
management of the cancer care system, the division of labor, 
responsibilities and resourcing are not entirely clear.
Figure 4 illustrates the key actors and areas in 
the national cancer treatment and research 
environment.
The national actors focusing on cancer operating on a 
statutory basis are the Cancer Registry and the National 
Cancer Centre. The Cancer Registry is a research institute 
focusing on cancer epidemiology operating in connection 
with the Finnish Cancer Society and is also a national 
registry operating under the responsibility of THL. The 
National Cancer Centre FICAN is a joint centre established 
by the Ministry of Health, Welfare and Sport for welfare 
regions and medical universities, with the aim of 
strengthening national cooperation and coordination to 
harmonise cancer prevention, diagnostics and treatment, 
to strengthen research and to ensure the availability, 
quality and equality of treatment throughout the country. 
FICAN's five regional centres work closely with university 
hospitals and their CC and CCC accredited (Cancer Center 
or Comprehensive Cancer Center according to the OECI 
classification).
In preparing and implementing the cancer 
strategy, strong consideration will also be given to 
existing national programs, projects and studies that 
cut across cancer prevention, treatment and research 
(Table 1).

European operating environment
Finnish cancer prevention, treatment and research are 
not only developed through national cooperation, but 
our activities are influenced by both European and 
global regulations, programs, recommendations, 
guidelines and cooperation networks. The field of 
cancer treatment and research in Europe has developed 
significantly in recent years through European Union 
initiatives, such as the EU's Action Plan on Cancer and 
the EU Mission on Cancer.
The EU's Action Plan against Cancer is a broad 
strategy that sets policy objectives for cancer 
prevention, early detection, quality care and support 
for survivors. The EU Cancer Mission supports the 
achievement of these objectives through research and 
innovation. The National Cancer Strategy supports the 
implementation of the EU's Action Plan against Cancer 
and the Cancer Mission in Finland (Table 2).

Equality in cancer strategy
In the Finnish healthcare system, the central goal of 
health policy is to provide citizens with equal access 
to preventive healthcare services and fast, high-
quality healthcare.
However, Finnish long-term studies have shown 
that the risk of developing cancer and cancer 
mortality vary in different population groups. These 
differences are strongly linked to cultural and social 
factors underlying health, such as ethnicity, mother 
tongue, level of education, age and health literacy. 
Inequality also occurs based on place of residence 
and economic situation. The current service system 
based on parallel primary health care services can 
also create and maintain differences in well-being 
and health. Finland is among the top countries in 
Europe in terms of the financial burden of cancer 
patients.5
Equality is a fundamental right enshrined in the 
Finnish Constitution. Formal equality means that all 
people are always treated in the same way and that 
everyone is offered the same services. However, it 
does not yet
guarantees the realization of equality. De facto equality 
(which can also be referred to as the need principle) 
means comprehensive and fair consideration of 
individual life situations, information about available 
support services and cooperation between different 
professionals so that every patient has the opportunity 
to achieve the same treatment outcome. In this strategy, 
the concepts of equality are based on the definitions of 
the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare6.
Achieving equity requires action. It strengthens 
the ability of health services and systems to identify 
and respond to the social and cultural factors that 
are linked to inequalities in care outcomes. Not 
everything for everyone, but to each person 
according to their individual needs.
The cross-cutting theme of the National Cancer 
Strategy is equality, which has been applied to all of 
the strategy's main objectives, ensuring that the 
strategy identifies the healthcare needs of all Finns – 
regardless of who they are or where they live.

Customer participation refers to the participation of 
service users in and influence on the service they 
receive or that of their loved ones. More broadly, it 
also refers to participation in and influence on the 
ideation, planning, development, implementation and 
evaluation of services, service chains and service 
entities, as well as health policy. The Act on the Well-
being Area emphasizes the participation of residents 
and service users7promoting opportunities for 
participation and influence.
Customers, patients and loved ones can be 
involved in the development of people-centered 
cancer care, for example as members of customer, 
patient or research committees, volunteers or 
experience actors, and as representatives of service 
users in welfare institutions.
Involving clients, patients and their loved ones in 
the development of services and their own care can 
improve the quality of services, adherence to 
treatment and the experience of cancer care. The 
perspectives of patients and loved ones are also 
valuable from the perspective of developing 
healthcare recommendations, planning research and 
developing health policy.
In addition to structures that promote inclusion 
and the interpersonal and interaction skills of social 
and healthcare professionals, participation requires 
sufficient health literacy among citizens.

PARITY
1.1 Customers, patients and loved ones 
participate in the development of cancer 
prevention, treatment and services
The involvement of customers, patients and loved 
ones in the development of healthcare and social care 
has been strengthened in recent years. Promoting 
participation is required in both international 
guidelines and domestic legislation. Through 
participation, preventive services and cancer 
treatment can be developed to be more people-
oriented and better meet the needs of customers and 
patients.
The participation of customers, patients and loved ones 
in the development of cancer care services is currently not 
implemented systematically, and there are regional and 
organizational differences in the opportunities for 
participation.
By taking into account the perspectives of service 
users, cancer prevention, screening and treatment 
pathways can be developed and citizens' participation 
in preventive services can be supported.
Target state

3.4 Functional capacity information 
guides the diagnostics and treatment 
of elderly cancer patients
The number of elderly cancer patients over 75 years of 
age is increasing rapidly, but assessment of the 
functional capacity of elderly patients and their 
participation in treatment decision-making are often 
inadequate. Elderly people are also underrepresented in 
clinical trials of cancer drugs, which is why scientific 
evidence on the tolerability and efficacy of drugs in 
elderly patients is insufficient.
Chronological age is not a reason to limit treatment. 
Functional capacity information assessed with a standardized 
measure should guide the cancer treatment plan.
Functional capacity refers to a person's physical, 
psychological, cognitive and social conditions to cope 
with meaningful everyday activities in their environment. 
Frail patients with impaired functional capacity should be 
identified through screening tests. Those with abnormal 
results are referred to a geriatric assessment, based on 
which an individual treatment and rehabilitation plan is 
drawn up. The aim of this is to maintain/improve 
functional capacity and possibly create the conditions for 
implementing cancer treatments.
Target state:
• Treatment decisions for the elderly are based on a comprehensive individual assessment of functional capacity. 
The patient participates in decision-making.
• The treatment and rehabilitation plan takes into account the treatment of the patient's other 
illnesses and geriatric syndromes, and the patient is referred to a nutritionist, physiotherapist or 
social worker if necessary.
• Diagnostic tests for functionally fit elderly patients are performed in the same way as for 
younger patients. Treatment decisions are based on a comprehensive assessment of 
functional capacity. In the case of frail patients, the benefit of invasive diagnostic tests is 
assessed in relation to the suffering they cause.
The achievement of the goal is measured as follows:
• The proportion of welfare areas where all eligible elderly cancer patients undergo 
geriatric oncological assessment (%).
• The proportion of welfare areas where primary health care has the opportunity to consult a 
geriatric oncology team already at the cancer diagnostic stage (%).
• Number of cancer treatment units in Finland that implement geriatric screening and 
assessment of elderly patients (%).
• Utilization of the G8 screen and/or the presence of a geriatrician in multidisciplinary care teams, monitoring 
the use of the G8 screen in different areas of cancer care.

Social Affairs, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Employment and the 
Health and Welfare, the Finnish Institute of Health and Welfare, the Finnish 
Institute of Social Affairs and Employment, the Finnish Institute of Labour and 
Social Affairs, the Finnish Research Council for Research and Innovation, 
foundations, patient organizations, and regionally (welfare regions, universities, 
regional associations). This strategy forms a strong foundation for practical 
implementation and guides the preparation of the implementation roadmap to 
be completed in 2026. The roadmap describes the responsible party(ies), 
resources, and monitoring for the implementation and monitoring of the 
strategy. In addition, the roadmap describes in more detail the implementation of 
the measures, the schedule, and the responsibility for the financing and 
implementation of each measure. National steering of the implementation of the 
strategy requires a strong mandate.
The goal is that all cancer stakeholders will participate in the 
implementation of the cancer strategy as part of the Cancer Mission 
Hub Finlandia to be established in 2026, including parties involved in 
cancer prevention, treatment and research and relevant ministries 
(Ministry of Health, Ministry of Education, Ministry of Employment and 
the Economy, Ministry of the Interior), authorities
(THL, Fimea, Palko), welfare areas and HUS, universities, 
polytechnics, FICAN, OECI-accredited CC/CCC centers, 
medical and nursing associations, Cancer organizations, 
Cancer Registry, patient organizations, foundations 
supporting cancer research and companies in the field.
The aim is to make greater use of FICAN regional 
centres and CC/CCC centres and the cooperation 
between them in five areas of cooperation. Division of 
labour, care chains and their knowledge-based 
management, training, securing competence, 
research networks and clinical trials are examples of 
tasks and responsibilities that can be developed more 
strongly in cooperation.
The implementation of the strategy also takes into 
account existing national programs, projects and 
studies that cut across cancer prevention, treatment 
and research (Table 1). The strategy also dynamically 
takes into account relevant initiatives, such as the lung 
cancer program being prepared under the leadership 
of Filha, to the extent that the goals and measures are 
consistent with the goals and measures of the cancer 
strategy.

measures and the savings they would generate, as well 
as private sector investments over the entire ten-year 
strategy period (with the exception of measures
